1. A Letter to Prospective Laboratory Members

Since earning my Ph.D. from the Department of Computer Science and Information Engineering at National Taiwan University in 2005 and joining the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at Yuan Ze University, more than twenty years have passed in the blink of an eye. I have always appreciated the classical saying: “A mountain need not be high; it becomes renowned if an immortal dwells within. Waters need not be deep; they become spiritual if a dragon resides beneath.” When people on the outside look at university laboratories, they sometimes judge first by institutional prestige or lavish hardware facilities. Frankly speaking, within the domestic environment, we are not a team born with massive funding or extravagant resources—and competition in international academia is even harsher and more intense than most imagine, pitting us directly against well-resourced leading groups worldwide. Yet it is precisely under such limited resources and conditions that generations of members in our laboratory have worked conscientiously and diligently day after day, accumulating progress step by step. Over the years, our work has been recognized with numerous major honors—including multiple University Outstanding Research Awards and National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) Research Awards—and together with cohorts of Ph.D., Master's, and undergraduate research students, we have published paper after paper in top-ranked international SCI Q1 journals. This proves that to conduct research in our field, what matters most has never been how many resources you hold, but how much effort, solid fundamentals, and execution you bring.

Regarding work ethic and research, Academician Der-Tsai Lee once shared a quote: “Every job is a self-portrait of those who did it. Autograph your work with quality.” Our laboratory is primarily an academic research-oriented laboratory. Once you step through this door, you must learn how to identify research questions worth answering, how to make effective use of the latest tools, and how to produce rigorous, reproducible experiments alongside logically clear academic presentations—making every piece of research you touch worthy of bearing your own signature.

Precisely because we cherish this hard-earned research environment, compared with the grades currently on your transcript or how strong your programming skills already are, what I value far more is your attitude and basic personal integrity. Technical gaps can be trained after you join; however, if someone works carelessly, cannot be found when expected to show up, merely wants a free ride on the resources built up through the diligent efforts of generations of senior lab members, or repeatedly creates trouble for me, then no matter how smart you are, we cannot walk on the same path.

Prof. Yu-Yen Ou · Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Yuan Ze University Bioinformatics Laboratory, Dept. of CSE, Yuan Ze University

5. A Research-Oriented Laboratory: Core Training

The Bioinformatics Laboratory is primarily an academic research-oriented laboratory. After joining the laboratory, members must build a solid foundation in the following three core competencies:

  1. Learning How to Conduct Research: Identify clear research questions from literature review, establish rigorous experimental designs and fair comparison baselines, and ensure the reproducibility of research results.
  2. Learning Academic Presentations and Scientific Writing: Train to write papers following a rigorous scientific structure and cultivate logically clear oral academic presentation skills, presenting research outcomes through explicit conclusions supported by figures, tables, and evidence.
  3. Making Effective Use of Research Tools: Proactively learn and utilize research assistance tools to improve execution efficiency, while genuinely understanding the operating principles of all code and methods, personally verifying every dataset and literature citation, and taking full responsibility for research quality.

6. Student Attitude Valued Most: Four Basic Requirements

Research methodologies and technical skills can be cultivated after joining the laboratory, whereas I place far greater importance on a student's attitude. Before joining the laboratory, please make sure you can commit to the following four points:

  1. Never create trouble for the advisor: Fulfill your proper duties as a student, strictly abide by academic ethics and regulations, and take responsibility for your own words, conduct, and research progress—never put your advisor in the position of handling problems that should never have occurred.
  2. Show up when you are expected to be there: Be punctual for regular laboratory meetings, scheduled discussions, and times requiring team coordination. If you have a legitimate reason, notify us in advance—never be absent without notice or become unreachable.
  3. Act with integrity and gratitude: Cherish the mentorship and assistance provided by your advisor and senior lab members rather than taking others' efforts for granted. Carry every committed responsibility through from start to finish, without making excuses or quitting halfway when encountering difficulties.
  4. Think about your contribution to the laboratory, rather than merely enjoying its resources: The laboratory's research environment, accumulated experience, and reputation are the collective achievements of generations of members. As a member of the team, you should contribute proactively, assist your peers, and pass down experience rather than merely enjoying existing resources.
